How to Identify a Rembrandt Painting

Authenticating a Rembrandt is notoriously difficult, even for experts. The Rembrandt Research Project spent decades reattributing works, demoting many paintings once thought to be his. Here is what specialists look for.

Stylistic Hallmarks

  • Chiaroscuro and tenebrism: Strong contrast between light and deep shadow, with figures emerging from darkness. Light is often a warm, golden glow falling on faces.
  • Psychological depth: The sitters look like real, thinking, feeling people, with subtle, introspective expressions rather than stiff poses.
  • Varied brushwork: Smooth, delicate detail in faces and hands, contrasted with loose, thick, almost sculptural paint (impasto) in clothing, jewelry, and highlights. His late work is especially rough and expressive.
  • Earthy palette: Browns, ochres, deep reds, and golds, with restrained use of bright colors.
  • Edges that dissolve: Forms blend softly into shadow rather than being sharply outlined.
